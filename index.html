Section 2
AI framework

AI in consumer marketing is a four-layer system — each layer building on the one before it. Without clean data, predictions are unreliable. Without predictions, agentic execution is directionless. Without governance, the system operates without accountability.

1
Data — always-on clean signals
A unified first-party data engine feeding real-time customer behavior into AI systems. The foundation everything else depends on — without it, predictions are guesswork and agentic execution fires at the wrong people with the wrong message.
CDP with real-time event streaming · First-party identity graph · Behavioral event taxonomy · Consent & preference management
AEOAdds a new signal type: AI share-of-voice tracking — measuring how often and how accurately your brand is cited in AI-generated answers, alongside traditional brand health and search signals.
2
Predictions — simulate before you spend
Predictive models that simulate LTV outcomes, churn risk, and channel elasticity before budget is allocated. Strategy becomes simulation-led — you know which bets are likely to pay off before making them, not after.
LTV prediction · Churn risk scoring · Marketing mix modeling · Channel elasticity simulation · Next-best-action engines
3
Agentic execution — scale what works
Human-configured AI agents that dynamically generate, test, and scale thousands of creative variants and interactions in real time. Humans set the strategy and guardrails — agents handle the execution volume and speed that no human team can match.
Dynamic creative optimization · Behavioral trigger automation · AI content generation · Conversational AI · Real-time bid management
AEOAdds a new execution surface: agents generating and maintaining structured content, schema markup, and entity signals at scale — ensuring AI systems consistently extract and cite your brand accurately across every answer engine.
4. Governance — runs across all three layers
Programmatic kill-switches, algorithmic bias monitoring, and strict human-in-the-loop validation layers. Not a fourth step — a horizontal constraint that applies at every layer. What makes the system trustworthy, not just powerful.
AEOGovernance now includes monitoring how AI systems describe your brand in generated answers — correcting inaccurate citations, flagging brand misrepresentation in LLM outputs, and ensuring your entity data is authoritative across AI platforms.

Section 4
The engineering advantage

AI gives every marketer access to powerful tools. What determines whether those tools deliver level-1 or level-5 outcomes is the architecture underneath them. A computer engineering mindset — systems thinking, data modeling, feedback loop design — is what closes that gap. This section shows what that looks like in each area of consumer marketing.

The core argument
Most marketing orgs stall at level 1 or 2 of AI maturity — not because the tools don't exist, but because the infrastructure underneath them isn't engineered properly. Clean data models, unified identity graphs, event-driven architectures, closed feedback loops — these are computer science problems, not marketing problems. A marketer who thinks like an engineer doesn't just buy AI. They design the system that makes AI work. The builds in this section are what that looks like in practice.
